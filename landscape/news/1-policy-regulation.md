# Policy and regulation

*Rolling, past two weeks. Last updated: August 7, 2026.*

What moved on rules, enforcement, and national strategy, and what it means for anyone selling or building AI.

## Europe
- **Enforcement went live.** As of August 2, the EU AI Office can investigate general-purpose model providers and levy fines up to 35 million euros or 7 percent of global annual turnover. ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai))
- **The hard parts slipped, transparency did not.** The Digital Omnibus deal pushed standalone high-risk rules to December 2027 and AI in regulated products to August 2028, but the Article 50 transparency obligations stayed on schedule, and the Commission published guidelines on how to meet them. ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/), [Commission guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-transparency-ai-generated-content))
- **The GPAI line hardened.** About 26 companies signed the EU Code of Practice, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, and Mistral. Meta refused and Chinese developers did not sign. ([EU AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/introduction-to-code-of-practice/))

## United States
- **Speed over rules.** The US finalized a set of voluntary AI safety tests rather than hard rules, keeping the innovation-first posture under AI czar David Sacks. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-finalizes-voluntary-ai-safety-tests-white-house-official-says-2026-08-03/))
- **A leadership gap.** The head of the US AI safety agency resigned about three months into the job, leaving the agency without a chief at an awkward moment. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/head-us-ai-safety-agency-resigns-2026-07-20/))
- **OpenAI wants rules it can live with.** The company publicly backed a mix of state and federal AI safety action, positioning itself as pro-regulation on its own terms. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/advancing-ai-safety-through-state-and-federal-action/))

## Canada
- **"AI for All."** Canada's national strategy builds on six pillars, with Minister Evan Solomon framing trust and sovereignty as the enablers, and Ottawa carried the same priorities to the G7 digital ministers meeting. ([Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news/2026/06/minister-solomon-highlights-canadas-national-artificial-intelligence.html))

## The geopolitics
- **US and China.** Analysts pressed on how Washington should handle AI talks with Beijing, and Europe kept positioning itself between the two on safety and trade. ([CFR](https://www.cfr.org/articles/how-trump-should-approach-ai-talks-with-china-targeted-dialogue-maximum-pressure), [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-ai-artificial-intelligence-safety-us-china/))

## What it means
For policy leads, the near-term work is the obligation that survived: Article 50 disclosure and documentation. The heavy high-risk protections are two years out. For sellers, the map of who signed the GPAI Code is the map of who is betting compliance is a selling point, and who is betting the opposite.

## How the big orgs respond, and where to read them
When a rule lands, watch what each company publishes, because that is positioning. Anthropic ([Transparency Hub](https://www.anthropic.com/transparency)), Microsoft ([Responsible AI](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/ai/responsible-ai)), Google ([AI responsibility](https://ai.google/responsibility/)), and IBM ([AI governance](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-governance)) all keep education surfaces, and the fast free breakdowns come from law firms like Gibson Dunn and CMS.
