# Sovereignty

**The trend.** Governments increasingly want to own their AI rather than rent it, and "sovereign" is becoming a procurement requirement, not a slogan.

**The evidence.** Canada's national strategy makes a sovereign AI foundation one of six pillars, and the EU frames its whole agenda around tech sovereignty, with its tech chief saying Europe's job is to build capability in AI, chips, and cybersecurity that people can trust. ([Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news/2026/06/minister-solomon-highlights-canadas-national-artificial-intelligence.html), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/12/5-takeaways-europe-tech-chief-henna-virkkunen-ai))

**Why it matters.** Sovereignty reshapes where enterprise AI money is allowed to go. Expect more national compute, national models, and buy-domestic pressure, which is both a constraint and an opening for vendors who can offer in-country deployment and control.

**Where it is heading.** Watch for sovereign-deployment SKUs and for "your data stays in your tenant, under your governance" becoming a standard line in enterprise pitches.
