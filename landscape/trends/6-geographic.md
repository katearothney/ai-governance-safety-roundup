# Geographic fragmentation

**The trend.** There is no single global approach to AI, and the gaps between the big jurisdictions are widening into three different trust philosophies.

**The evidence.** The EU leads with hard rules and fines, live as of August 2. The US leads with speed, finalizing voluntary tests and resisting a single federal standard while a state-by-state patchwork grows. Canada leads with trust and sovereignty. ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai), [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-finalizes-voluntary-ai-safety-tests-white-house-official-says-2026-08-03/))

**Why it matters.** A product sold globally now has to carry a different trust story into Brussels, Washington, and Ottawa. One global deck gets the message wrong in two of the three. This is a real cost that only grows as more countries pick their own points on the line.

**Where it is heading.** Watch whether US state law hardens into a fifty-flavored compliance burden, and whether the EU model gets copied or resisted elsewhere.
