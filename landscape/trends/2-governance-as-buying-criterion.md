# Governance as a buying criterion

**The trend.** Governance moved from a cost you pay to a reason a buyer signs. The moat is shifting from which model you use to whether you can prove it is governed.

**The evidence.** The EU AI Act put real fines behind non-compliance, and the GPAI Code of Practice split the market into companies that signed (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Mistral, about 26 in all) and companies that refused (Meta). That split is a positioning choice, not a default. ([EU AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/introduction-to-code-of-practice/))

**Why it matters.** A provider that can hand a regulated buyer a clean compliance story turns a cost center into a close. This favors whoever built the safety and documentation muscle years ago rather than bolting it on this summer. It is the single most important shift for how AI gets sold this year.

**Where it is heading.** Expect "provably governed" to become the headline claim, and expect buyers to start asking for the framework, the third-party standard, and the evidence, not just the promise.
